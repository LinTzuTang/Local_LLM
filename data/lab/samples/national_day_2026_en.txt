My fellow citizens, this city upon a hill is built atop a chain of guardian mountains. It is the splendid outcome of democratic governance which grants us a solid basis for peace of mind. A city upon a hill, therefore, should have more than just a dazzling skyline; it must stand on a firm foundation.
The well-being of our people is the very foundation of Taiwan. National progress lies not merely in output value and exports, but in increasing benefits and pay for our workers, alleviating the burden on families, providing care for the older generation, and giving our youth the confidence that their hard work will pave the way to a brighter future.
We are therefore taking a pragmatic approach to governance, promoting higher salaries, lower taxes, increased benefits, and reduced burdens so that each and every individual in this nation can stand steady and live well.
To address the declining birthrate, we are building upon our national childcare policy for ages 0–6, and have proposed a new population strategy for Taiwan in the area of family support. This includes increased subsidies, lighter loads, greater flexibility, and expanded care from the stages of childbirth and childrearing to education, making support for families even more comprehensive.
Starting next year, we are on track to launch NT$5,000 monthly allowances for every child, from birth until age 18. By combining this with maternity benefits and subsidies for assisted reproduction, affordable childcare, family-friendly workplaces, and social housing for newlyweds and families of young children, we will ensure that our young people will no longer be forced to choose between work and family.
Families should not have to raise children all on their own. To support a family is to safeguard Taiwan’s future.
As we are now a super-aged society, we are advancing the national policy for a Healthy Taiwan, which ranges from cancer screenings, smart healthcare, and in-home care to improving working conditions for healthcare professionals and increasing the nursing workforce. This way, our people will live not only longer, but healthier.
Our Long-term Care 3.0 plan, which launched this year, further integrates healthcare with social welfare, the community, and technology. This allows older adults to receive care in a familiar setting, and gives the “sandwich generation,” who must look after both older and younger family members, greater support and less hardship.
To ease housing pressure, we will continue implementing our program to support 1 million renter households, and will expand supply through a variety of methods, including government construction of social housing, subleasing and management programs, rental subsidy programs, as well as housing units allocated to the government through newly added urban renewal and floor area ratio incentives. We are also increasing the proportion of social housing for newlyweds and families with young children, implementing our Preferential Housing Loans for the Youth 3.0 program, and raising the maximum loan amounts for these families. Stable homes are not only necessary for us to thrive in our careers; they are also what young people need to take the leap in starting families and pursuing their dreams.
To address labor and talent shortages, we have also rolled out the Cross-border Workforce Enhancement Program. We firmly maintain that local workers are our primary workforce, while the cross-border workforce serves in a supplementary capacity. We will increase local employee wages in tandem with migrant worker quotas, allow for greater retention of experienced migrant workers seeking re-designation as skilled foreign workers, and promote direct government-to-government recruitment.
We have also amended the Act for the Recruitment and Employment of Foreign Professionals, expanding opportunities for international talent and foreign students to come to and stay in Taiwan. This will address corporate staffing shortages while enhancing employee benefits and pay, ensuring that talent is not just willing to come, but willing to stay.
